TUMBLE MARKETING NOTES
=======================


DISCORD ANNOUNCEMENT POST ("lookwhatimade" channel)
-----------------------------------------------------
Tumble, a slower camera you can own

Just shipped this. It's live on the App Store now.

Tumble is a camera app built around a limit instead of an algorithm. You get
twelve shots a day, a "roll" that resets every morning. No unlimited
shooting, no ads to unlock more, no feed disguised as a camera.

The twist: your photos don't show up instantly. You shake your phone to
"develop" them (or press and hold if you've got reduce motion on). Prints
land in a scattered pile called the Drawer instead of a grid, and they age
over time, picking up grain, vignette, a little patina.

Photos stay on-device. No account, no photo cloud, no ad tracking. Free to
start, with optional one-time unlocks for a bigger roll.

Nostalgic film looks (think faded instant, warm archive tones) are already
cooking and landing in the next update soon.

Grab it here: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tumble-instant-camera/id6788386458

Wait for it.


INSTAGRAM STRATEGY (budget: Rs. 500)
-----------------------------------------------------
Given the tiny ad budget, organic content is what actually matters. Ads are
a small boost on top of proven organic content, not a standalone campaign.

Content pillars (Reels-first, that's where reach comes from):
1. The shake-to-develop moment - blank print, shake, image resolves. Best
   scroll-stopping hook, reuse with different photos/music.
2. Before/after grain - flat phone photo cut to the aged, vignetted print.
3. The Drawer as physical metaphor - slow pan across scattered pile of
   prints, like dumping out a shoebox of old photos.
4. Contrast narrative - "your camera roll" (doomscroll grid) vs. "Tumble"
   (calm twelve-shot roll). Text-on-screen, no voiceover needed.
5. The constraint story - "You get 12 shots a day. That's it." Framed as a
   feature, inherently shareable/debatable in comments.
6. Founder-voice posts - building an app that pushes back against
   infinite-scroll, on Instagram itself. Lean into that tension.
7. Filter previews (once faded-instant/warm-archive/soft-memory ship) -
   carousel comparing the three styles on the same photo.

Cadence: 3-4 Reels/week, 1 static carousel/week.

Ad spend plan for Rs. 500:
- Don't open Ads Manager and split budget across a formal campaign, the
  overhead eats a tiny budget.
- Post organically for a few days first.
- Pick the best-performing Reel (saves/shares matter more than likes).
- Use the in-app "Boost post" button on that single Reel (not a new
  campaign). Target interests: film photography, disposable camera, VSCO,
  analog photography. Age 18-34, target country/city.
- Run the boost for 3-5 days (not 1 day) so delivery has time to optimize.
- Basically: let organic performance pick the winner, then push proven
  content further with the budget.


HOW TO CREATE THE VIDEOS (no editing experience needed)
-----------------------------------------------------
1. Capture footage
   - In-app moments (shake-to-develop, Drawer, roll counter): use iPhone's
     built-in Screen Recording (Settings > Control Center > add "Screen
     Recording", then swipe down, tap record).
   - Physical "hands holding the phone" shots: use a second phone/camera to
     film the first phone's screen and the shaking motion. Natural light
     near a window, plain background, hold the filming phone steady.

   IMPORTANT: screen recording alone does NOT do justice to the shake
   feature. It only shows the on-screen reveal (print resolving), not the
   physical shake that causes it. Combine both:
     - External shot: hand shaking the phone (from a second phone/camera).
     - Screen recording: the develop animation on the phone itself.
     - Cut between them (1-2 sec each) or use picture-in-picture with the
       external shot as the main frame and the screen recording inset.
   This shows cause (shake) and effect (image resolves) together, which is
   what actually sells the feature.

2. Edit with CapCut (free, iOS/Android, beginner-friendly)
   - Import clips, trim to the good parts.
   - Add text overlay hooks (e.g. "You only get 12 shots today"), keep on
     screen 1-2 sec.
   - Add a trending/calm sound from CapCut's built-in library.
   - Keep total length 8-20 sec (loop clips like the develop animation can
     be 3-5 sec).
   - Export vertical 9:16 for Reels.

3. Example first video
   - Screen-record: open app, take a shot, shake to develop.
   - Trim to just the shake -> reveal moment in CapCut.
   - Add opening text: "You have to wait for it."
   - Add a calm/lofi trending sound underneath.
   - Export and post as a Reel.


CAROUSEL POST IDEAS (static photo posts)
-----------------------------------------------------
1. "How it works" walkthrough (for people who haven't seen the app):
   - Slide 1: Hook, "A camera with only 12 shots a day"
   - Slide 2: Screenshot of the Roll/shot counter
   - Slide 3: Screenshot of a print mid-develop (blank -> resolving)
   - Slide 4: Screenshot of the Drawer (scattered pile)
   - Slide 5: "Download on the App Store" CTA

2. This vs. that comparison (high save/share format):
   - Your camera roll: unlimited shots, cloud sync, a feed, an algorithm.
   - Tumble: 12 shots, on-device, no feed, no account.

3. Manifesto / text-only carousel (plain background, big typography):
   - "Your camera roll has 40,000 photos."
   - "You remember none of them."
   - "Tumble gives you twelve a day."
   - "You'll actually look at these."

4. Privacy claims, one per slide:
   - "No account." / "No photo cloud." / "No ad tracking." / "Photos stay
     on your phone."

5. Filter showcase (once faded-instant/warm-archive/soft-memory ship):
   - Same subject shot in each of the three filters, one per slide, last
     slide the unfiltered original for contrast.

6. Real prints from the Drawer (once there are early users):
   - Curated actual user photos, grain/vignette visible. Ask early users
     for permission to repost as UGC.

Priority order: start with #1 and #3 (don't need real user content or
shipped filters yet), then move to #5 and #6 once filters ship and users
exist.
