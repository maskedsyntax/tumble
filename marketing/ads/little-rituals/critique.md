## Highest-impact fixes applied

1. Compared each source against its relevant grade candidates and replaced one-size-fits-all styling with Warm Archive, Everyday, Date Stamp, Pool Party, and scene-specific color treatment according to light and texture.
2. Gave each image a subject-driven reveal—steam lift, stem diagonal, sleeve edge, rain bands, and flash—so the middle behaves like directed shots rather than a repeated before/after template.
3. Removed the rounded dawn mask visible in the first frame and replaced it with a clean horizontal develop front tied to the steam direction.
4. Tightened scene entrances from 12 to 7 frames and exits from 18 to 10 frames, reducing accidental double-exposure overlap while keeping match-cut continuity.
5. Brought look labels in eight frames earlier and protected a 3.5-second animated CTA landing, improving muted product clarity and the final action hold.

## User-feedback revision — softer diner

The original Flash Night selection was semantically appropriate for the direct-flash source, but the rendered result crushed the booth shadows and made both faces unnecessarily hard. A second comparison covered Original, Everyday, Disposable, Date Stamp, Camcorder, Golden Hour, Warm Archive, Faded Instant, Overcast, Hazy Flare, Light Leak, Sunbleached, and Pool Party.

Everyday replaced Flash Night because it keeps the late-night warmth and light grain while retaining facial, hair, jacket, and booth detail. The brief white flash remains only as the transition cue; it no longer determines the final color grade.
