# Concepts

Three different ideas — not three palettes.

## Concept A — City in Motion
- Strategy: transformation
- Hook: THE CITY DOESN'T POSE
- Core idea: five kinds of city motion become five distinct film looks.
- Visual metaphor: each filter reveal rides the direction of the subject—crowd, train, traffic, bicycle, and light trail.
- Emotional intent: restless, nocturnal, observant, alive.
- Product demonstration: an honest raw/graded split in every scene.
- Pacing: five 2.3-second velocity studies, then a 3.5-second brand landing.
- Typography: one huge hook, compact filter labels, restrained end-card copy.
- Transitions: whip cuts, directional seams, parallax push-ins, and moving light streaks.
- Ending / CTA: five graded city strips collapse behind the Tumble icon and App Store CTA.
- Why it could work: the proof is immediately legible while the cinematography speaks the visual language of Reels creators.
- Asset feasibility: complete; five generated sources plus real Tumble renders.
- Execution risk: still-image motion can feel artificial if the camera travel is too large; keep it directional and controlled.

## Concept B — One Second, Five Eras

- Strategy: demo
- Hook: ONE CITY. FIVE ERAS.
- Core idea: one skyline remains fixed while five looks flash across it in rhythmic bands.
- Visual metaphor: a city block becoming a film contact sheet.
- Emotional intent: satisfying, graphic, collectible.
- Product demonstration: repeated filter changes on identical pixels.
- Pacing: faster cuts and longer look-name holds.
- Typography: large years/era language, minimal body copy.
- Transitions: hard color wipes and match cuts along building edges.
- Ending / CTA: pull out to show the full set and Tumble mark.
- Why it could work: strongest controlled comparison.
- Asset feasibility: complete.
- Execution risk: less visual variety and less explicit subject motion.

## Concept C — Transit Loop

- Strategy: curiosity
- Hook: KEEP UP WITH THE CITY
- Core idea: one moving white line connects train, traffic, bike, and skyline like a transit map.
- Visual metaphor: the line is both route and developing seam.
- Emotional intent: kinetic, connected, metropolitan.
- Product demonstration: the route line converts raw areas into graded areas.
- Pacing: continuous forward travel without visible scene resets.
- Typography: station-like filter-name markers.
- Transitions: match motion and line morphs.
- Ending / CTA: route terminates at the Tumble icon.
- Why it could work: highly ownable motion system.
- Asset feasibility: complete.
- Execution risk: complex transitions may compete with the before/after proof.
