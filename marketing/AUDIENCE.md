# Audience

## Primary

iPhone photographers in their late teens through thirties who like disposable
cameras, imperfect personal snapshots, photo dumps, and tangible keepsakes.

## Secondary

Parents, partners, and friend groups who want ordinary moments to feel more
personal than the default camera roll.

## Frustrations

- Modern phone photos often look technically clean but emotionally anonymous.
- Camera rolls become huge archives that are rarely revisited.
- Many camera apps sell generic preset subscriptions or over-polished effects.

## Desires

- Photos with texture, warmth, and a sense of time.
- A small ritual that makes taking and keeping a photo feel deliberate.
- Privacy and ownership without another account or subscription.

## Jobs to be done

- Give an everyday snapshot a coherent film character.
- Make today's casual moments feel collectible.
- Keep and share a small, personal archive.

## Alternatives they already use

The iPhone Camera and Photos apps, disposable-camera apps, preset editors,
instant cameras, and social photo dumps.

## Objections

- "The source photo was already beautiful."
- "This is just a CSS filter."
- "The effect is too aggressive or fake."
- "I do not want another subscription or photo cloud."

## Emotional motivations

Nostalgia for ordinary life rather than staged vintage cosplay: dinners,
driveways, birthdays, late nights, and summers that only become meaningful
after time passes.

## Vocabulary they actually use

Film look, disposable, grain, warm, faded, flash, point-and-shoot, memory,
photo dump, camera roll, private, pay once.

## Situations where they need this

Dinner with friends, a family birthday, a late-night goodbye, a quiet weekend
task, a community-pool afternoon, and other moments too ordinary to stage.
